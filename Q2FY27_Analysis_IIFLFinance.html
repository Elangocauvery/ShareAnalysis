Key correction: not a merger with 360 ONE — it was a demerger

IIFL Finance did not merge with 360 ONE. The reverse happened: in 2019 the old IIFL Holdings split into three listed companies — (1) the lending business stayed and the company was renamed IIFL Finance; (2) the wealth business became IIFL Wealth Management, renamed 360 ONE WAM in November 2022; (3) broking became IIFL Securities, now IIFL Capital Services. Holders of 7 IIFL Holdings shares received 7 IIFL Finance + 7 IIFL Securities + 1 IIFL Wealth share. The merger that did happen was the NBFC subsidiary India Infoline Finance folding into IIFL Finance (made effective March 2020).

Timeline

The RBI embargo and the comeback — gold-loan AUM (₹ crore)

4 Mar 2024: RBI barred new gold loans over gold-purity assaying and auction lapses; lifted 19 Sep 2024. The gold book halved from ₹26,081 cr to ₹12,162 cr and is now ₹65,729 cr — 2.5× the pre-embargo level.

Blackstone — where things stand

What happened
7 Sep 2026: media reports (Bloomberg) said Blackstone is among global investors in talks with Fairfax and other shareholders to buy a stake. Talks described as preliminary.
Why Fairfax may sell
Fairfax is pursuing IDBI Bank; regulatory norms push it to rearrange holdings in other lenders. It also plans to fold IIFL Capital (and Digit) into the IDBI platform.
Company's response
9 Sep 2026: IIFL Finance told exchanges it is not party to any such negotiation; price moves are market-driven. No deal has been announced or signed.

Blackstone's Indian financial-sector record: Federal Bank 9.99% (~$705 m, 2025), Aadhar Housing Finance buyout (2019), bid for 20% of Axis Finance (2025).

Coming equity — the dilution maths

  • Board, 27 Jun 2026: enabling approval to raise up to ₹10,000 cr (QIP/rights/preferential); GMTN limit doubled to $2 bn.
  • AGM, 24 Jul 2026: passed with 85.66%, but 41.73% of public institutions voted against.
  • Management flagged 10–20% dilution. At ~₹600, the full ₹10,000 cr would be ~16.7 cr new shares ≈ 39% of ~42.5 cr existing shares — so the full enabling amount is unlikely in one go.
  • If a strategic investor took fresh shares via a preferential issue, it could address both the Fairfax overhang and the capital need — a possibility only, not announced.

Cash flow (FY26, consolidated, ₹ crore)

For a lender a negative operating cash flow is normal: new loans count as an operating outflow. FY26 CFO of −₹13,976 cr is the loan-book growth, funded by ₹17,843 cr of net borrowing/equity (CFF). That is why funding cost, ratings and liquidity are an NBFC's real lifeline.

Strengths

  • One of the top-2 NBFC gold lenders; 3,000+ gold branches
  • 90% secured; GNPA 1.5%, PCR 91%
  • Co-lending with 18 banks — ₹1.7 lakh cr originated since FY14
  • Record liquidity ₹13,805 cr, CRAR 23.5%

Weaknesses

  • 2024 RBI embargo — governance scar remains
  • Standalone CRAR 17% — growth needs capital
  • High gold concentration (~53%)
  • Volatile assignment income (₹28 cr vs ~₹350 cr a year earlier)

Opportunities

  • Formalisation of the unorganised gold-loan market
  • Affordable-housing momentum (disbursement +10% QoQ)
  • AI: 85% of SME LAP underwriting, voice AI in gold sales
  • Entry of a strategic investor

Threats

  • Gold-price correction; tighter RBI gold-loan norms
  • Competition from banks, Muthoot, Manappuram
  • MFI stress returning
  • Dilution and Fairfax overhang
