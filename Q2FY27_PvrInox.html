Two rivers, one river: PVR + INOX

Rivals for 20 years → COVID blow → merger in 2022 → net cash in 2026.

Timeline

1978 / 1997
Bijli family buys Priya Cinema, Delhi; 1997 JV with Village Roadshow opens India's first multiplex, PVR Anupam at Saket.
1999 / 2002
INOX Leisure (Jain family's INOX Group) set up in 1999; first multiplex in Pune, 2002.
2006
Both list on the stock exchanges.
2010–2018
Acquisition race: INOX bought Fame and Satyam; PVR bought Cinemax (138 screens), DT Cinemas and Chennai's SPI Cinemas (₹850 cr).
2020–21
COVID: theatres shut for months; OTT viewing habits grew fast.
27 Mar 2022
Merger announced: 3 PVR shares for every 10 INOX shares. PVR 871 + INOX 675 = 1,546 screens.
12 Jan / Feb 2023
NCLT Mumbai approval; PVR INOX Ltd from Feb 2023. Ajay Bijli MD, Pavan Jain Chairman.
FY24–FY26
Loss-making screens shut (85 → 72 → 18 a year); popcorn business Zea Maize sold Jan 2026; pivot to capital-light growth.
Jun–Sep 2026
Net cash; ₹303 cr buyback; record quarter.

Why they merged — 6 reasons

  • COVID losses and debt — hard to carry alone.
  • OTT threat: cinemas needed scale and a premium experience.
  • Bargaining power with mall landlords, distributors and advertisers.
  • Cost synergies: F&B buying, technology, ad sales and overheads combined.
  • Overlap: the two stopped bidding against each other for the same malls.
  • One balance sheet to push into Tier 2/3 towns.

Which lever matters most? (impact on quarterly EBITDA)

  • ATP +₹10 → ~₹39 cr more ticket revenue; ~₹22 cr EBITDA after film hire.
  • SPH +₹10 → ~₹44 cr F&B; ~₹34 cr EBITDA — F&B is the stronger lever.
  • Occupancy +1 pt → ~1.5 mn more admits; ~₹38 cr EBITDA (CB calc).

Do they own the buildings, or rent them?

  • Lease liabilities ₹6,020 cr (Mar 2026) — 12× the ₹501 cr bank debt. This is the "hidden debt".
  • Rent ~₹294 cr a quarter ≈ ~14% of revenue (CB est.).

  • Why lease? Crowds are in malls; buying land locks capital and cuts ROCE.

Story of success

How PVR INOX got to net cash

  • 1 · Shut loss-making screens boldly (175 in three years).
  • 2 · Used developers' money for new screens — own capex fell.
  • 3 · Sold non-core (popcorn brand) to repay debt.
  • 4 · ATP/SPH growth + fixed-cost control → free cash.

Who is ahead? PVR INOX on screens and pipeline; Cinépolis is moving fast on FOCO; local chains lead Tier 3 on price.

Where PVR INOX sits in the film value chain

Producer → distributor → exhibitor (PVR INOX) → audience → OTT/TV. Domestic theatrical = 63% of film income.

What the sector needs

  • More screens: 6.8 per million — a tenth of China.
  • Cheaper screens: low-cost formats for small towns.
  • Steady content: 1,972 releases in 2025 (+8%); Tamil 315, Telugu 313.
  • Protected theatrical window — ~8 weeks before OTT.

Where PVR INOX plays

  • Exhibition: the core (~97% of revenue).
  • Distribution: PVR INOX Pictures distributes Hollywood and regional films (₹60 cr/qtr).
  • F&B and ads: own income inside the cinema.
  • Production: small; not a core focus.
