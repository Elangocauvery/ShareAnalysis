Q2 FY27 at a glance

The one-line read

Core business was strong: operating revenue grew 16.3% and operating profit 15.7% on higher yields (57.5 bps) and 14.4% QAAUM growth. Reported PAT grew only 6.1% because other income (treasury gains) collapsed from ₹180.80 crore in Q1 to ₹16.91 crore. The real watch-point is not this quarter's profit — it is the drop in equity net-flow market share and costs running ahead of revenue.

Industry context — September 2026 (AMFI)

Industry AUM fell 5.1% month-on-month to ₹82,64,116.63 crore (₹82.64 lakh crore) on 30 Sep 2026 from ₹87.08 lakh crore at end-August, driven by ₹1,83,155.76 crore of debt-fund net outflows (quarter-end corporate liquidity and advance-tax withdrawals from liquid/money-market funds). SIP contributions still rose 0.6% to a record ₹32,490 crore, and equity fund net inflows rose to ₹38,307 crore from ₹29,329 crore. Year-on-year, industry AUM is still up 9.3%.

Why this matters less for ICICI Pru AMC's profit than the headline suggests: debt and liquid funds earn a small fraction of what equity schemes earn per rupee. The money that left was low-fee money; the money that kept coming (SIP, equity) is high-fee money. Also, ICICI's QAAUM is a quarter average, so the late-September dip will show up mostly in Q3 averages — and debt balances usually return after quarter-end.

Why other income fell — the MTM effect

ICICI Pru AMC parks its surplus cash, plus SEBI-mandated seed capital, mostly in units of its own mutual fund schemes. Under Ind AS these are valued at market price at every quarter-end (fair value through profit and loss), so any rise or fall goes straight into other income even if nothing is sold. April–June 2026 was a rising market, giving a large paper gain of ₹180.80 crore; July–September was volatile and weaker, so the same holdings added only ₹16.91 crore. The company does not split other income into its parts, but the swing is most likely the mark-to-market (MTM) piece, which is notional and non-cash.

Is it like inventory for a manufacturer?

Similar in one way: both are revalued at quarter-end and can hit profit without cash moving. But inventory is part of the operating cycle and is carried at the lower of cost or realisable value — it can only be written down. These securities are surplus treasury money, carried at fair value both up and down, and sit below operating profit. The closer comparison is a manufacturer parking surplus cash in mutual funds — its MTM swings would land in other income in exactly the same way. An AMC has no real inventory because its product is a service.

Two caveats
  • "Notional" cuts both ways: in a sharp correction this line can turn negative and pull reported PAT below core PAT.
  • For an AMC, treasury and business move together: falling markets mean lower MTM now and lower AUM and fees later.

      Balance of the argument

      The long-run engine — Indian household savings moving into mutual funds, with ICICI as the top active and hybrid manager backed by ICICI Bank's distribution — is intact. Brokers project roughly 14–15% revenue and PAT CAGR over FY26–28. The stock already prices quality (41.5x trailing earnings), so returns from here depend on earnings growth more than re-rating. The two things that would break the thesis are a lasting loss of equity flow share and regulatory fee cuts; both are visible early in monthly AMFI data.

            • ICICI = faster growth, higher yield, bigger alternatives leg, but costs up 17.9% and a premium price (41.5x).
            • HDFC = best cost control and cheaper (~35x), but slower AUM growth and flat share.
            • Market rewarded the cost beat (HDFC +5.1%) and punished cost creep (ICICI −2.0%).
            • Learning, not advice: decide which trade-off fits your own conditions.
