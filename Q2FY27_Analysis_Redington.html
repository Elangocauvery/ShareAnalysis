Overview

In one line: the biggest quarter in Redington's history — revenue up 48% to ₹43,002 crore, led by AI server / GPU large deals of over ₹3,800 crore. The catch: AI deals carry thinner margins, short-term borrowings more than doubled in six months to fund working capital, operating cash flow turned negative, and the Turkey unit (Arena) is still losing money.

Business Model

The business in 60 seconds: Redington makes nothing. It is a giant technology wholesaler: it buys from brands such as Apple, Lenovo, HP, Dell, Microsoft, AWS and Google and sells to thousands of resellers, system integrators, retailers and data-centre builders across India, the Middle East, Africa and Turkey. On every ₹100 of sales it keeps about ₹4.9 of gross margin and ₹1.35 of net profit. So the whole game is volume × how fast the money turns (working capital).

Revenue formula: Revenue = units shipped × selling price, across four business groups → Gross margin ≈ 4.9% → less operating costs (~2.9% of sales) → EBITDA ≈ 2% → less interest and tax → PAT ≈ 1.35%. Return on equity = thin margin × very high capital turnover.

Where Redington sits in the AI money chain

1. Chip & server makersGPU makers and server OEMs. Highest margins in the chain.
2. Redington (distributor)Sources, finances, imports, configures and delivers the hardware. Earns a low-single-digit margin on a very large ticket.
3. System integrators / partnersDesign and install the cluster for the end customer.
4. Neo-clouds & data centresRent GPU capacity. Nearly all of Q2's large deals came from here.
5. Enterprises & AI usersBuy compute, AI PCs, cloud and security software.

Management sizes India's neo-cloud capacity moving from 1.5 GW to 7 GW and then 12 GW.

AI beyond servers

  • AI PCs: above 40% of commercial PC sales, about 10% of consumer.
  • Cloud & cybersecurity (SSG): +39%, all units grew, gross margin above 6% — the better-quality AI-linked revenue.
  • AI Exchange: partner marketplace, hundreds of partners engaged; management calls it early and long-term.
  • AI Centre of Excellence: physical centre in Singapore plus a virtual one.
  • CloudQuarks 2.0: cloud platform rolled out across the Middle East and Africa.
  • Five AI learning centres in India under CSR.

Questions that decide whether the AI trade creates value

  • Do large deals repeat every quarter, or was Q2 a bunch-up? Management guides H2 growth "north of 25%", well below Q2's 48%.
  • Does the margin on AI deals stay below 4.5%, and does the share of such deals keep rising? If yes, group margin drifts lower even as profit grows.
  • Do neo-cloud customers pay on time? Receivable provisions were ₹213 crore this quarter (₹67 crore SISA, ₹135 crore Arena).
  • Reports say AI server prices may rise more than 15% from early 2027 — that lifts ticket size but also the capital locked per deal.
  • Chip makers are starting to finance neo-clouds directly; watch whether that changes the distributor's role.

Arena (Turkey) – the drag

  • Revenue down 37% after exiting Turkish-lira businesses and one vendor contract; new management team in place.
  • Redington's share of Arena's Q2 loss: ₹115 crore, including business loss, transition cost and about ₹40 crore of the ₹80.33 crore impairment of intangibles.
  • Without Arena: EBITDA ₹1,003 crore (+58%), PAT ₹657 crore (+55%), debt-to-equity 0.45×.

Working capital ↔ CFO ↔ cash conversion

  • In distribution, each extra day of working capital at this sales run-rate locks about ₹470 crore† of cash.
  • Four extra days year-on-year plus 48% higher sales together explain the cash absorbed.
  • Cash conversion (CFO ÷ operating profit before working-capital changes): −135%† in H1 FY27 against +63%† in H1 FY26.
  • Fast growth eats cash first and returns it when growth slows — provided receivables are collected. That makes collection quality the thing to watch.
  • Management: debt lines are sufficient for now; if strong growth persists it may consider more debt, moderating dividend or raising equity.

What would change this view
  • Operating cash flow turning positive while growth stays above 25% → cycle and trigger scores improve.
  • Arena reaching break-even → the biggest positive trigger moves to "delivered".
  • Two quarters of falling device revenue, or large-deal value dropping sharply → scores fall.

SWOT

Strengths

  • Scale: annualised sales run-rate above ₹1.7 lakh crore†
  • Presence across devices, servers, cloud, security in 30+ markets
  • ROE 21.6%, ROCE 22.7%
  • Operating costs growing slower than sales (+40% vs +48%)
  • Long vendor relationships; Apple 30%, Lenovo 10%

Weaknesses

  • Net margin only about 1.35%; small slips hurt
  • Operating cash flow −₹2,571 crore in H1
  • Arena (Turkey) losing ₹115 crore a quarter (Redington's share)
  • Apple is 30% of revenue
  • No promoter; largest holder Synnex at 24.12%

Opportunities

  • India neo-cloud / data-centre build-out: ₹1.5 lakh crore over four years (management)
  • AI PCs: above 40% of commercial PCs
  • Cloud and cybersecurity subscriptions with margin above 6%
  • GCC-Levant +121%, Africa +35%

Threats

  • Demand falling after 30–50% device price increases
  • West Asia conflict: logistics, insurance, liquidity
  • AI capex pull-back; neo-cloud credit risk
  • Receivable provisions (₹213 crore in Q2)
  • Chip makers financing customers directly
