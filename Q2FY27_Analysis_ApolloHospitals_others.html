Five things that defined the quarter

1. Beds are filling and prices are rising
Apollo occupancy 73%, Max 77%, Fortis 72%, Yatharth 72%. Revenue per patient (ARPP) is up 7–8% at the large chains.
2. A big wave of new beds
Apollo targets 13,800 beds by FY31; Aster 15,070; NH 7,800+; Medanta 3,450+ greenfield beds. New units lose money at first — Apollo's new hospitals posted a ₹35 cr EBITDA loss.
3. Private equity is pouring in
Advent → Yatharth ₹3,150 cr (24.9%); Blackstone co-controls the merged Aster DM Quality Care; IHH wants >50% of Fortis; Temasek-backed Manipal raised ₹9,275 cr in its IPO.
4. Cancer-drug price cap is the overhang
NPPA capped trade margins at 30% on 137 cancer drugs from 9 Nov 2026. Oncology-heavy chains (HCG, Max, Apollo's pharmacy arm) are most exposed.
5. Who pays the bill matters
At Apollo insurance is 46% of inpatient revenue, self-pay 39%, government/PSU 9%. Rainbow is 63% cash. Max cut its institutional (government-scheme) share from 21.5% to 18.4%.

Why a new hospital loses money first — the J-curve

Doctors and nurses are paid from day one; patients and insurance empanelment arrive slowly. Real examples this quarter: Medanta Noida's first EBITDA-positive quarter (₹8.6 cr); Yatharth Faridabad Sec-20 broke even in nine months; Jupiter Dombivli's loss shrank from ₹9.5 cr to ₹0.2 cr; Max's new brownfield beds broke even within a quarter. The curve shown is illustrative, not a company forecast.

The payer profit ladder

1. International patient
Highest tariffs, complex cases, paid upfront
2. Self-pay (cash)
List price, immediate collection
3. Insurance / TPA
Negotiated rates, 30–45 day lag, big volumes
4. Govt scheme (CGHS/PMJAY)
Fixed package rates, long delays, thin margins — but fills beds

This is why Max cut its institutional share from 21.5% to 18.4% and Aster shifted 2 points from walk-in to TPA payers.
